Where Chicagoland's Third Places Are (and Aren't)

"Third places" are the informal public spaces where people gather outside of home and work — cafes, libraries, community centers, social facilities. This maps every one of them across 319 towns and cities in Cook, DuPage, Kane, Lake, McHenry, and Will counties, and shows which places actually have the most, relative to how many people live there.

Third places per 10,000 residents

Raw counts favor big cities by default — Chicago will always have the most cafes just by having the most people. Dividing by population instead shows which towns are relatively well-served, regardless of size.

        Worth remembering

        A town showing zero here means OpenStreetMap has no cafe, library, community center, or social facility tagged inside its boundary — not necessarily that none exist. Some of these are genuinely underserved; some may just be under-mapped. Both are worth knowing.

        Methodology

        Geography: 319 Census-designated places (incorporated towns/cities) across Cook, DuPage, Kane, Lake, McHenry, and Will counties, from 2020 TIGER/Line boundaries.

        Third places: OpenStreetMap features tagged amenity=cafe, library, community_centre, or social_facility, pulled via the Overpass API, August 2026.

        Population: 2023 5-year American Community Survey estimates, by place.

        OpenStreetMap data changes constantly — this is a snapshot, not a permanent record.
