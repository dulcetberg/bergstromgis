Worth knowing

This map jumps between 21 real benchmark years, 1492 through 2010, plus a modern 2026 frame built from current Census boundaries, rather than smoothly morphing shapes between them. Borders didn't actually move gradually, they changed through specific wars, treaties, and purchases, so faking a smooth animation between snapshots would invent a precision that never existed. Indigenous nations are shown with dashed borders throughout the entire timeline, not just before 1776, because the land didn't stop being theirs when European powers drew lines on paper claiming otherwise.

Methodology

Boundaries: 21 benchmark-year snapshots (1492-2010) from the Historical Basemaps project (GPL-3.0), clipped to a North American extent. The final 2026 frame uses real current U.S. Census Bureau TIGER/Line state boundaries, not a repeat of the source's last snapshot, since state lines haven't changed since Hawaii's 1959 statehood.

Coloring: each colonial or national power gets a fixed color across the entire timeline. Indigenous nations share a color family but are individually named polygons with dashed borders, distinguishing "Indigenous nation" from "colonial/national power" without relying on hue alone. Click any territory for its real name.
